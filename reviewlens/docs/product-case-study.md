# Product case study

Product managers need to distinguish recurring complaints from memorable anecdotes. ReviewLens supports investigation through imports, corpus statistics, scoped questions and traceable evidence. It does not automatically decide a roadmap.

The first release covered CSV/JSON validation, statistics, source inspection, comparison, history and exports. The next increment adds no-key semantic retrieval because API billing blocked the owner's evaluation. Generated interpretations remain a separately paid option.

## Acceptance criteria

Preserve valid rows; calculate exact counts and denominators; isolate owners; keep citations inside scope; show exact quotations; distinguish generated prose, rule labels and retrieved evidence. Device indexing should require no key, preserve completed progress and fail with an actionable fallback.

## Proposed success measures — not achieved results

- Time to identify and verify the leading complaint versus manual reading.
- Analyst-rated useful-answer rate.
- Retrieval recall@5 on independently labelled questions.
- Unsupported-claim rate for the separate generation path.
- Initial download/index time, subsequent query latency and abandonment.
- Cost per completed investigation, separating provider calls and local computation.

Measure a baseline and agree thresholds before claiming business impact. No real-world accuracy, time-saving percentage, revenue or adoption is asserted.

## Ownership

Saahil Gupta defined the portfolio objective, requested the browser product, reported validation results and raised the no-payment constraint. Codex assisted with implementation, architecture, tests and documentation. The original supplied prototype was reviewed and the hosted surface ported to TypeScript for its runtime. This is evidence of product scoping, acceptance design, cost-aware decisions and iteration; it is not evidence of managing an enterprise engineering team.
